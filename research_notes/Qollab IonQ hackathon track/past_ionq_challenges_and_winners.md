# Past IonQ-Sponsored Hackathon Challenges, Their Winners, and Prior Georgia Tech / Qollab Events (2021-2026)

Research date: 2026-10-07. Scope: IonQ hackathon challenges from 2021 to 2026 and the projects that won them, to work out what judges reward. It also covers Qollab (qollab.xyz), the co-organizer of the Oct 9-11, 2026 Qollab x IonQ hackathon, and earlier Georgia Tech quantum hackathons.

Disambiguation: **Qollab** (qollab.xyz, "Quantum creativity, in the open", an open-source quantum creative community) is NOT **Qolab** (qolab.ai, a superconducting-hardware company in Madison, WI, co-founded by John Martinis) — [search result showing qolab.ai newsroom](https://qolab.ai/newsroom); [Qollab site](https://qollab.xyz/explore/creative-challenge).

## Key Question 0: What has Qollab co-sponsored with IonQ, and what Georgia Tech quantum-hackathon history exists? (most relevant to the Oct 2026 event)

### Takeaway
Qollab has run two IonQ-backed **creative open calls**: a Fall/Winter 2025 Creative Challenge, won by "Quantum Garden" and "QAVE", and a Spring 2026 funded cohort. It is now co-organizing with IonQ a distributed global 48-hour hackathon, Oct 9-11, 2026, and **Georgia Tech is one of the university nodes**. Its rubric (Novelty & Creativity, Technical Execution, Impact Potential, Documentation Quality) and its "beyond the tutorial bar" rule echo IonQ's iQuHACK creative challenges. Unlike iQuHACK's in-person tracks, judges run projects **on a free simulator, and hardware results neither add nor subtract points**. I found no earlier Georgia Tech-branded quantum hackathon.

### Cited Findings
**Qollab x IonQ Quantum Hackathon (Oct 9-11, 2026): the target event**
- Qollab and IonQ bill it as "the largest distributed, global quantum hackathon", a "48 hour race to develop open-source quantum projects worth forking for". Registration ran Sept 28 to Oct 8 (11:00 ET), the build runs Oct 9 19:00 ET to Oct 11 19:00 ET, and global winners are announced the week of Oct 19 — [Qollab hackathon overview](https://qollab.xyz/programs/hackathon)
- **Georgia Tech is a listed node.** Other nodes include UCLA, CMU, NYU Tandon, Dartmouth, U Toronto, Waterloo, Sherbrooke, Aalto, U Barcelona, FIU and more. "Quantum Computing Association" (the name of GT's quantum club) appears among the "participating entities" — [Qollab hackathon overview](https://qollab.xyz/programs/hackathon); GT's club is called the "GT Quantum Computing Association" — [GT CoC events listing](https://www.cc.gatech.edu/events/2023/05/11/gt-quantum-computing-association-weekly-meeting)

  — [Rules & terms](https://qollab.xyz/programs/hackathon/terms)
- Prizes:
  - Local (nodes with 10+ submissions): 1st $500 + $5,000 compute; 2nd $300 + $3,000; 3rd $200 + $2,000.
  - Global: 1st $10,000 + $20,000 compute; 2nd $5,000 + $15,000; 3rd $2,500 + $10,000.
  - Specialty awards (listed at $500 + $5,000 compute): Most Novel/Creative, Best of each theme, Most Impact, Best Documentation.
  - The global top 3 also get three 1:1 IonQ-engineer mentorship sessions and expedited IonQ interview access.
  - Up to 5 projects per team, each judged separately.
  — [How it works](https://qollab.xyz/programs/hackathon/how-it-works)
- If a node has fewer than 10 submissions, its top 3 advance straight to global judging with no local awards. Each node can also forward one project per specialty category — [Rules & terms](https://qollab.xyz/programs/hackathon/terms)
- Mentor areas Qollab names: "QAOA and variational methods, error mitigation, chemistry, finance, optimization, tooling" — [Mentors page](https://qollab.xyz/programs/hackathon/mentors)
- Press coverage calls it "Quantum's biggest build weekend" and labels it "SESSION 2026.10.09" — [Quantum Zeitgeist](https://quantumzeitgeist.com/ionq-quantum-hackathon-qollab-host/)

### Inferences
- The Qollab rubric looks like a cleaned-up version of IonQ's iQuHACK creative-track criteria: "how quantum + how cool" (2023), "beyond randomness" (2024), and "educational clarity" (2025). Expect IonQ judges to penalize RNG-only projects and tutorial clones, and to reward a quantum mechanism that is essential to the experience.
- Hardware runs earn nothing directly at Qollab. Their value is in the story: real data and noise analysis help Technical Execution and Documentation, but what scores is a project that runs cleanly on the free simulator and fits one IonQ job per run.
- The past Qollab winners split into a **creative artifact** (Quantum Garden) and a **rigorous educational visualizer** (QAVE). These map directly onto the Playground and Unlocked themes. Documentation and remixability are core values, and Best Documentation is a separate award.
- The Georgia Tech node is likely to be small, possibly fewer than 10 submissions. If so, its top 3 advance directly to global judging with no local cash awards (per the rules above).

## Key Question 1: What kinds of problems does IonQ pose at hackathons, and how open-ended are they?

### Takeaway
IonQ alternates between two formats, often in the same year. (A) **Open-ended creative "use the QPU to make X" prompts**: a game in 2022, "generate something new" in 2023, and a quantum sci-fi story in 2024. (B) **Tightly specified, auto-graded optimization or puzzle challenges** with resource penalties: quantum image classification in 2023 (remote), quantum-state "vault hacking" in 2024 (remote), MaxCut ansatz design with varQITE in 2025, and an entanglement-distillation network game in 2026. Since 2025 IonQ's MIT challenges have moved toward format (B), with leaderboards and rules that penalize resource use (gate count, Bell pairs).

### Cited Findings
**MIT iQuHACK 2021 (Jan 2021; MIT's second annual; remote)**
- 219 participants (from 943 applicants) formed 49 teams. IonQ and D-Wave provided hardware. **Team 47 won the IonQ challenge.** QCB ("Quantum Whack-A-Mole") was the overall winner — [iQuHACK 2021 post-event README](https://github.com/iQuHACK/2021)
- Team 47's "Quantum Supremacy" is a multiplayer game where "the players are qubits": each picks a secret initial state, players take turns adding rotating or entangling gates, they get non-projective measurement glimpses, and the highest ⟨σz⟩ wins — [2021_Team-47 repo](https://github.com/iQuHACK/2021_Team-47)
- The overall winner, QCB's "Quantum Whack-A-Mole", is an educational 2-qubit game for young students to learn to build circuits that maximize amplitude — [2021_QCB repo](https://github.com/iQuHACK/2021_QCB)

**MIT iQuHACK 2022 (Jan 28-30, 2022): IonQ + Microsoft joint challenge**
- Prompt: "we'd like you to use a quantum computer to build a game, or a component of a game." Suggested ideas: a QRNG or noise source for procedural generation, "quantum logic itself as a gaming mechanism", or QML applied to gameplay. Teams had access to a real IonQ trapped-ion QPU through Azure Quantum (Q#, Qiskit or Cirq). The organizers advised "circuits that use under a few dozen two-qubit gates" — [2022_microsoft_ionq_challenge README](https://github.com/iQuHACK/2022_microsoft_ionq_challenge)
- Deliverables: a public GitHub repo with README, results screenshots, presentation, and an optional blog post — [same](https://github.com/iQuHACK/2022_microsoft_ionq_challenge)
- Prizes: $500 Visa card for the top team and $250 for the next four. All 5 winners got to present to the Microsoft Quantum team — [same](https://github.com/iQuHACK/2022_microsoft_ionq_challenge)
- Results: **Division winner: Opti-maze.** Second place: "Qic Qac Qoe", "QuHackJack Casinos™", "Soulinq", "Wyn". Creativity award: "QuanTris". Social good award: "QuackAttack". The event had 400 participants, 75 projects and 57 countries — [iQuHACK 2022 page](https://www.iquise.mit.edu/iQuHACK/2022-01-28)
- **Opti-maze** is a competitive game teaching QAOA and CO2-emission reduction. In standard mode the player flips binary variables to beat the quantum computer's result; in cooperative mode the player "guides the quantum computer rather than competes with it" by having QAOA solve chosen subsets. The team (PhD and master's students from Zurich, Milan, Radboud and MPQ) used Qiskit on Azure with the IonQ backend but had only "a tiny exposure to the real hardware" — [IonQ blog](https://www.ionq.com/blog/february-09-2022-iquhack-winners-opti-maze)

**MIT iQuHACK 2023 (Jan 27-29, 2023): IonQ "Trapped Ion Applications", in-person + remote**
- In-person prompt: "you must use a quantum computer to *generate* something new". The ideas given were quantum music, a quantum ray tracer, procedural world generation and QGANs. Teams were told to debug on a simulator, then the noisy simulator, then hardware. "We will judge your entry based on both (1) how quantum it is and (2) how cool it is." A write-up (blog, video or social post) "is heavily considered during judging" and must report hardware access details: shots and backends — [2023_IonQ README](https://github.com/iQuHACK/2023_IonQ)
- Remote prompt ("Mr. Feynman's crashed spaceship"): **quantum image classification on Fashion-MNIST.**

  - Part 2 (70%): a classifier circuit appended to the encoder, scored by accuracy × 0.999^(number of 2-qubit gates).
  - "Preference will be given to creative solutions with a minimal amount of classical processing" ("degree of quantumness").
  — [2023_IonQ_Remote challenge PDF](https://github.com/iQuHACK/2023_IonQ_Remote)
- Results:
  - **IonQ in-person:** 1st Entanglement5, 2nd "let me know, no worries!"

  - The event had 300 in-person attendees, 1.2K remote hackers and 159 projects (42 in-person) — [iQuHACK 2023 page](https://www.iquise.mit.edu/iquhack/2023-01-27); [Qrabs PR](https://github.com/iQuHACK/2023_IonQ/pull/9)

**MIT iQuHACK 2024 (Feb 2-4, 2024)**
- In-person prompt: **"What if __ is Quantum"**, a sci-fi story (novel, animation or interactive game) in which an everyday concept follows quantum rules.
  - "Humor and Creativity: ... we value humor and creativity more [than aesthetics]."
  - "The development of your story should be directly influenced by a real quantum computation."

- Remote prompt: **"Hacking is what we do"**, a 13-vault quantum-state inversion game.
  - Each vault key is a secret state |ψk⟩=Uk|0⟩. Teams submit probe circuits Up (20 probes per vault, 500 shots each) and then an attack circuit UA so that ⟨0|UA Uk|0⟩ → 1.

### Inferences
- IonQ's creative prompts are **highly open-ended** but share one constraint: the quantum part must be *meaningful*. The guidance became more explicit each year: "how quantum it is" (2023), "beyond randomness" (2024), and "essential ... beyond simply generating randomness" (Qollab 2026).
- IonQ's structured prompts are **narrow and leaderboard-driven** (fixed functions, fixed API, auto-grading), and they almost always include a **resource penalty**: 0.999^(2-qubit gates) in 2023, α = 4ng/(4ng + x) in 2024, minimal gate count in 2025, and the Bell-pair budget and success probability in 2026. Resource efficiency is a recurring IonQ value, consistent with NISQ-era hardware realities.
- Recurring IonQ themes include optimization (QAOA, MaxCut, varQITE), state preparation and learning (vault inversion, MPS, graph states), QML/encoding (image classification), quantum networking and error filtering (distillation), and quantum-simulation-flavored storytelling. The same authors (Daiwei Zhu, Jason Iaconis) wrote the 2024 prompts and are named in the 2025 Discord clarifications, so IonQ's applications team sets the house style: humorous framing with rigorous scoring.

- No official 2025 IonQ-track winner list exists. The A*STAR "top prize" may or may not be the IonQ remote track.

## Key Question 2: What do winning IonQ projects have in common?

### Takeaway
Creative-track winners tie a **specific quantum mechanism to a domain metaphor** (entanglement as trait correlation, partial-SWAP as migration, entanglement as networking, long-range spin interactions), wrap it in a **game or interactive narrative**, and document it well. Several winners explicitly use or mention IonQ-specific traits such as all-to-all connectivity. Real hardware runs help but are not strictly required: 2022's winner barely touched the QPU. Structured-track winners win on **leaderboard efficiency**: fewer gates and fewer Bell pairs for the same fidelity, plus adaptive strategy and persistence.

### Cited Findings
- **2023 1st (in-person), Entanglement5, "Game of Finches"** (Pieter-Jan Stas, Zixiao Xu, Aniruddha Sen, Sophie Li, Sasha Geim): simulates the evolution of Darwin's finches.
  - Each finch has 4 trait qubits. Each generation runs a circuit of: initialization by single-qubit rotations, intra-finch controlled rotations ("entangle the characteristic qubits"), inter-finch partial SWAP (migration between islands), and a second intra-finch step.
  - The output distribution plus natural-selection pressure seeds the next generation.
  - "This evolution algorithm tries to model the dynamics ... while harnessing the full extent of the all-to-all connectivity of the IonQ platform."
  - **Run on IonQ Harmony QPU** (50 shots per circuit). It reframes noise as a feature: "imperfect gate fidelities are partially an advantage ... adds another layer of randomness, typical in evolution."
  - Deliverables: interactive notebook, slides, PDF documentation and visualizations.
  — [Entanglement5 PR/README](https://github.com/iQuHACK/2023_IonQ/pull/3); [fork](https://github.com/ZixiaoXu/2023_IonQ); [UMass CICS](https://www.cics.umass.edu/news/cics-student-wins-iquhack-2023)
- **2024 2nd (in-person), FalQon, "Your Wonderland, Alice"**: "What if PROFESSIONAL NETWORKING was quantum??" Each candidate contact is a qubit whose personality is encoded with random parametrized rotations. Entangling gates represent connections, one added per time step and weighted by personality, career position and "home-company stock price". Success is read from the average qubit state. The repo includes an interactive ASCII CLI game, simulations, circuit and trend plots, and a presentation PDF — [FalQon PR](https://github.com/iQuHACK/2024_IonQ/pull/5); [FalQon repo](https://github.com/NelsonOoi/2024_IonQ_FalQon)
- **2024 1st (in-person), fATCAT**: the submission links a Google Slides deck. Its backend notebook builds Trotterized time evolution of a 2-spin model with **power-law couplings J ~ 1/r^α (α = 1.13)** plus transverse field and tunable disorder W, the kind of long-range Ising model native to trapped ions. It runs on the Qiskit Aer simulator, and a "scar" folder holds about 180+ generated figures and density data. My reading of the code is that the story was driven by a physically meaningful trapped-ion-style spin simulation rather than an RNG. The narrative itself is in the slides, which I did not access — [fATCAT PR](https://github.com/iQuHACK/2024_IonQ/pull/4)
- **2022 winner, Opti-maze**: an educational and social-good framing (QAOA plus CO2 reduction) with two gameplay modes, from a team of PhD and master's researchers. Hardware exposure was minimal ("a tiny exposure to the real hardware") — [IonQ blog](https://www.ionq.com/blog/february-09-2022-iquhack-winners-opti-maze)
- **2021 IonQ winner, Team 47**: a multiplayer game where "the players are qubits", built to give "intuition for gate-model quantum computing, quantum entanglement, and measurement" — [2021_Team-47](https://github.com/iQuHACK/2021_Team-47)
- **2026 winners**:
  - 2nd-place iQuackers combined known distillation protocols **adaptively** — [The Tech](https://thetech.com/2026/02/19/iquhack-2026)
  - 1st-place BYU Q-gars credited stamina: "all five of us worked the entire night" while other teams took shifts — [BYU Universe (via search snippet)](https://universe.byu.edu/campus/byu-students-win-mit-quantum-hackathon-in-debut-appearance)
  - A non-placing participant repo (team "Quantum Verse") shows the competitive pattern: a 2-pair "Phase Protection" protocol for Z-dominant edges, a 3-pair X+Z protocol for mixed noise, and graph heuristics for expansion — [aj123451/MIT-iQuHACK-2026-IonQ](https://github.com/aj123451/MIT-iQuHACK-2026-IonQ)
- **Qollab winners** (Quantum Garden, QAVE): one polished, hardware-backed creative artifact and one rigorous, synchronized educational visualizer, both remixable with docs — [Quantum Garden](https://qollab.xyz/explore/quantum-garden); [QAVE](https://qollab.xyz/explore/qave)
- **Non-IonQ-specific signal:** at iQuHACK in-person tracks, projects "will be judged on your project's performance on an actual quantum computer" — [iQuHACK 2023 page](https://www.iquise.mit.edu/iquhack/2023-01-27); [iQuHACK 2025 page](https://www.iquise.mit.edu/iquhack/2025-01-31)

### Inferences
- **Mechanism-to-metaphor fidelity wins creative tracks.** Winners pick a quantum effect whose math mirrors the domain: correlation becomes entanglement, mixing becomes partial SWAP, connection strength becomes entangling angle, and disorder becomes disorder. A rotation used only as a dice roll does not win.
- **Interactive or game form is the most common winning wrapper.** 2021 (Team 47 and the overall winner QCB), 2022 (Opti-maze), 2023 (Finches) and 2024 (FalQon's CLI game) were all games or interactive simulations.
- **IonQ-native features are a differentiator.** All-to-all connectivity (explicitly cited by Entanglement5), long-range spin interactions (fATCAT's α-power-law couplings), and native MS/ZZ-type entangling gates show judges that the team understands the platform.
- **Hardware runs strengthen but do not guarantee a win.** Entanglement5 ran on Harmony, while Opti-maze barely did. At Qollab hardware is explicitly unscored, so use it for documented insight (noise vs. simulator comparison), not as the main selling point.
- **Treating noise as a feature, or explicitly analyzing it, is valued.** Examples are Entanglement5's noise-as-mutation idea and the 2026 adaptive noise filtering.
- **Research-grade teams often win.** The winners included PhD and grad-student teams (Opti-maze, Entanglement5) and the strongest remote team in 2025 (A*STAR researchers). A clear technical core matters more than flash.
- **Documentation and presentation weigh heavily**: the 2023 write-up was "heavily considered", 2025 required "educational clarity", 2026 had 10-minute presentations, and Qollab has a Best Documentation award.

### Gaps
- Judges' stated reasons for specific wins are almost never published. The IonQ blog on Opti-maze gives none, and the 2023/2024 winner pages give none.
- Details of the 2023 remote winner (ioncoffee), the 2024 remote winners (Quick, PerimeterTeam), and the 2026 BYU solution code were not found.
- fATCAT's narrative (in Google Slides) was not accessed. Its description above is inferred from code only.

## Key Question 3: What judging rubrics have IonQ challenges used?

### Takeaway
The only full published rubric is iQuHACK 2022's: five criteria at 20% each (Utility, Technical Merit, Creativity, Relevance, Communication), plus Social Good for a separate award. IonQ's own prompts since then add "how quantum + how cool" (2023), humor and creativity with real computation beyond RNG (2024), auto-graded scores with resource penalties (2023 remote through 2026), and educational clarity (2025). Qollab 2026 uses four traits: Novelty & Creativity, Technical Execution, Impact Potential and Documentation Quality.

### Cited Findings
- **iQuHACK 2022 rubric (Microsoft+IonQ and QuTech)**, each criterion scored 0-10:
  - (a) **Utility of Concept** ("not limited to quantum computing applications, but could also include educational utility"; for incomplete work, "consider the utility of the intended outcome")
  - (b) **Technical Merit** ("how well does the approach ... exploit technical knowledge of concepts in quantum mechanics and quantum computing")
  - (c) **Creativity** ("surprising, inventive, or otherwise 'outside of the box'")
  - (d) **Relevance** to the challenge (for MSFT+IonQ: "Build a game or a component of a game")
  - (e) **Communication** (write-up and presentation)
  - (f) **Social Good**
  - Challenge Winner score = 20% each of (a) through (e). The Creativity award weights (c) at 50%; the Social Good award weights (f) at 50%. Required submissions: a short write-up (README, notebook tutorial or slides) and a GitHub link.
  — [iQuHACK 2022 Scoring Rubric (Google Doc)](https://docs.google.com/document/d/e/2PACX-1vR5PVoInN_Fi42lIOchhblgGBPblgNyouj1XHukonZ4sdqY-p5ulS9TxdzvddEcDNFc5k_6teFyKzXv/pub); linked from [2022 challenge README](https://github.com/iQuHACK/2022_microsoft_ionq_challenge)
- **2023 in-person (IonQ):** "(1) how quantum it is and (2) how cool it is". The write-up is "heavily considered" and must cover the problem, approach, simulation and hardware results, and hardware usage (shots, backends) — [2023_IonQ](https://github.com/iQuHACK/2023_IonQ)
- **2023 remote (IonQ):** a quantitative score of fidelity or accuracy × 0.999^(2-qubit gates), with Part 1 worth 30% and Part 2 70%. "The final score will take into account the degree of quantumness" — [2023_IonQ_Remote PDF](https://github.com/iQuHACK/2023_IonQ_Remote)
- **2024 in-person (IonQ):** humor and creativity over aesthetics, a real quantum computation that drives the story, advanced phenomena or a clever use of simple ones, and "beyond randomness" — [2024_IonQ](https://github.com/iQuHACK/2024_IonQ)
- **2024 remote (IonQ):** overlap ⟨0|UA Uk|0⟩ × α, where α = 4ng/(4ng + x), averaged across vaults, on a live leaderboard — [2024_IonQ_Remote](https://github.com/iQuHACK/2024_IonQ_Remote)
- **2025 (IonQ):** count of shots at the optimal cut for each of the three variants, minimal gate count, no classical solvers, a bonus for a unified approach, and "educational clarity of your explanations". In-person teams also give a presentation they must be able to defend — [IonQuHack2025.ipynb](https://github.com/iQuHACK/2025-IonQ/blob/main/IonQuHack2025.ipynb)
- **2026 (IonQ):** in-game score from utility qubits held, with claim strength = F·p/√rank, plus a 10-minute presentation to sponsor-mentor judges — [game_handbook.md](https://github.com/iQuHACK/2026-IonQ/blob/main/game_handbook.md); [The Tech](https://thetech.com/2026/02/19/iquhack-2026)
- **QCHack 2022 Azure/IonQ:** teams had to complete both parts (the standalone tasks plus their own problem explored on the IonQ simulator and QPU) to qualify to win — [Microsoft Q# blog (via search snippet)](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/qsharp/azure-quantum-challenge-at-qchack-2022-recap)
- **NQCC 2024:** "evaluated for scalability and for how its application could address challenges in healthcare, energy, and engineering" — [IonQ news](https://ionq.com/news/ionq-qpus-and-classiq-software-power-winning-projects-at-the-uks-national)
- **Qollab x IonQ 2026:** Novelty & Creativity, Technical Execution, Impact Potential, Documentation Quality, plus the "beyond the tutorial bar" gate that can zero out Novelty and Technical — [How it works](https://qollab.xyz/programs/hackathon/how-it-works); [Rules & terms](https://qollab.xyz/programs/hackathon/terms)
- **Qollab Creative Challenge 2026 review:** "Creativity and originality; Technical feasibility; Clarity of idea and potential impact; Alignment with our open, accessible ethos" — [Creative Challenge page](https://qollab.xyz/explore/creative-challenge)

### Inferences
- Mapping 2022 onto Qollab 2026: Creativity becomes Novelty & Creativity, Technical Merit becomes Technical Execution, Utility/Social Good becomes Impact Potential, and Communication becomes Documentation Quality. "Relevance" turns into theme fit for the specialty awards. The criteria have been stable for five years, so a project designed for 2022's rubric would score well at Qollab.
- Equal weighting (20% each in 2022) means a balanced project beats one that maxes out a single axis. The separate specialty awards (Creativity at 50% in 2022; Most Novel and Best Documentation at Qollab) are the exception.
- Since Qollab judges press "Run", a broken or slow notebook directly hurts Technical Execution and Documentation, and the one-IonQ-job-per-run limit matters.

### Gaps
- No published numeric weights for the Qollab four-trait rubric.
- No published rubrics for 2023-2026 iQuHACK beyond the challenge text; the in-person scoring sheets are not public.
